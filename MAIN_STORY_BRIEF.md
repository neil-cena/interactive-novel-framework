# MAIN STORY BRIEF — Equivalent Ashes (Full Game)

This document contains story-specific content for the full-length main story, to be developed after the worldbuilding, tone, and systems are locked in from the current short story (Equivalent Ashes). It is **not** a worldbuilding document — it references `WORLD_BIBLE.md` for all lore, systems, and canon. None of the elements documented here appear in the current short story.

For the current short story, see `EQUIVALENT_ASHES_BRIEF.md`.

## Chapter 1: Main Story Arc

### 1.1 Overall Structure

1. Establish rules, cultures, and history of the world
2. A small number of anomalies occur — easily missable
3. Conflicts within the established world occur (no rules broken)
4. Anomalies become more noticeable — rumors of magic returning
5. Discovery of a philosopher's stone user (main antagonist)
6. Defeat of the antagonist
7. Brief peace
8. True revival of magic
9. Final alchemy vs magic battle — alchemists might use a philosopher's stone
10. The reveal of the Entity and peak power of the cult
11. **Confrontation with the Entity — containment, negotiation, or redirection, not direct combat.** The Entity cannot be destroyed. It can only be managed.
12. Peace at a massive cost

### 1.2 Key Structural Notes

- The philosopher's stone user is a **"false answer"** — defeating them does NOT solve the anomalies. The real problem is deeper.
- The Entity should feel like **"it's been there the whole time"** — not a sudden reveal but a slow, creeping realization.
- The final conflict with the Entity is about **containment, negotiation, or redirection** — not "fight god." The Entity cannot be destroyed. It can only be managed.
- The arc moves from: Local problems → Hidden pattern → False solution → True threat → Existential reckoning.

### 1.3 Gameplay Loop

For each location/scenario:

1. Arrive at location
2. Observe (worldbuilding through environment, not exposition)
3. Talk to factions (get competing perspectives)
4. Identify who is paying the cost in this situation
5. Make a decision

**Narrative architecture:** This story is built according to the four-layer architecture specified in [`NARRATIVE_ARCHITECTURE.md`](NARRATIVE_ARCHITECTURE.md). See that document for all structural rules. Story-specific architecture notes are in Chapter 2 below.

## Chapter 2: Main Story — Architecture Notes

This section documents how the four-layer architecture applies to the full main story. It is a planning document for future development, not a final specification.

### 2.1 Layer 1 — The Main String

The 12 beats in Chapter 1.1 are the candidate pearls for the main string. Not all of them are necessarily pearls — some may be hub nodes that most players pass through but that could theoretically be approached from different angles. The following are confirmed pearls (cannot be skipped by any path):

- Beat 1 (world establishment) — the player must be grounded in the world before anything else is legible
- Beat 5 (philosopher's stone user discovered) — the false answer must be confronted
- Beat 7 (brief peace) — the false resolution must be felt before it collapses
- Beat 10 (Entity revealed) — the true threat must be named
- Beat 11 (confrontation with the Entity) — the story's central argument is resolved here
- Beat 12 (peace at massive cost) — the cost of resolution must be witnessed

Beats 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, and 9 are likely hub nodes — important but approachable from multiple angles via long spokes and state-gated routes.

### 2.2 Layer 2 — Scale Implications

The main story covers multiple cities, factions, and years of in-world time. Hub-and-spoke structure at this scale means:

- Each major location functions as its own hub cluster
- Long spokes may span entire cities or faction headquarters
- Alternative strings at this scale represent genuinely different lives (joining a faction, leaving the republic, going into exile) rather than single-story diversions
- The commitment point for alternative strings may be a season of in-world time, not a single choice

### 2.3 Layer 3 — State at Scale

World stats at the main story scale track larger historical forces:

- Faction power balances (CEA dominance, Freehands reach, Red Balance influence)
- Magic return intensity (anomaly frequency, breach size)
- Public awareness of the Entity
- The protagonist's accumulated reputation and relationships

State from the short story (Equivalent Ashes) does not carry over to the main story — the main story is set later, with a different scope. However, the short story establishes the *kind* of protagonist the player understands themselves to be. The main story's opening should be informed by what that kind of protagonist would have become.

### 2.4 Layer 4 — Alternative Strings at Scale

At main story scale, alternative strings represent full alternative narratives that could each sustain their own shorter game. Examples:

- *The Faction Path:* The protagonist fully commits to one faction's ideology and pursues their solution to the Entity threat. Each faction offers a different alternative string with a different theory of how to handle the crisis. Most end in soft closure (the protagonist's faction wins their internal battle but the world cost is massive) or hard closure (the faction's theory was wrong and the protagonist dies for it).

- *The Exile Path:* The protagonist concludes that Caldris cannot solve this and leaves the republic entirely — to Thornfeld, the Free Ports, or Valdmere — seeking knowledge or resources unavailable at home. This is a long alternative string with its own arc and its own ending that has nothing to do with Caldris's political resolution.

- *The Private Life Path:* The protagonist finds a person, a place, or a craft worth more to them than the crisis. They settle. The world resolves without them — at great cost. The protagonist is alive, in a particular place, having chosen a particular life. This is the main story's equivalent of the "settling down" soft closure from the short story.

All alternative strings at this scale follow the same rules as at the short story scale. The difference is scope, not structure.
