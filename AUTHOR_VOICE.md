# Author voice — Equivalent Ashes (warm table, dark world)

## Purpose

This document formalizes how we write: a **dark setting** with **optimistic trajectory**, **subtle humor**, and a **human, collaborative narrator**—like a friend who happens to be a good Dungeon Master. It exists so revisions and new content do not drift toward monotone, “professional,” or oppressively grim prose.  

## North star

- The world can be harsh; the **stance** is forward-leaning: agency, connection, small wins, and quiet grace under pressure.
- The reader should feel **safe enough at the table** to care, laugh softly, and keep going—not bludgeoned by tone.
- The voice should convey the themes of the story.

## Non-negotiables

1. **Split contract:** brutal or bleak facts are allowed; **narration stays humane**—clear, embodied, not clinical.
2. **Optimism is trajectory, not denial:** hope shows up in **true details** (what people do next, what they refuse to give up), not slogans.
3. **Humor is pressure-release, not a stand-up set:** wit, irony, understatement, situation—**light touch**, context-earned.
4. **Anti-report-speak:** avoid incident-report / corporate-neutral voice; prefer plain verbs, sensory specifics, and occasional asides.

## Axes (theme vs tone)


| Axis         | What it is                                             | What it is not                                               |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Theme**    | Dark facts, cost, risk, moral weight                   | Grimdark as aesthetic-only; cruelty without meaning          |
| **Tone**     | Warm, collaborative, “we’re in this together”          | Monotone professionalism; cold omniscient briefing           |
| **Humor**    | Dry, situational, gallows grace                        | Constant levity; punchlines that erase stakes                |
| **Optimism** | Agency, connection, small wins, orientation after pain | Toxic positivity; telling the reader everything will be fine |


## Narrator stance: the “DM friend”

- **Clarity first:** the player-reader always knows what’s happening and why it matters.
- **Empathic framing:** acknowledge cost and fear without wallowing; **validate cost, then return agency** (“you could…,” “the move that’s yours”).
- **Intimacy:** occasional **asides** and second-person or direct-address beats where the engine allows—tight, not chatty.
- **Pacing contrast:** after tension, a breath—humor, humanity, or a small true thing—not a flat summary.
- **Hope without spoiling:** orient toward what’s *possible* next; don’t flatten mystery or consequence.

## Humor playbook

- **Understatement** after intensity.
- **Irony** that respects the scene (characters and world stay serious; the *telling* can be dry).
- **Situational** comedy from constraints, habits, mismatches—not random quips.
- **Punch-up / sideways:** aim humor at situation, institution, or shared absurdity—not at victims or at the reader’s investment.
- **Quota:** if every paragraph jokes, pull back; if none do for a long stretch in a human scene, consider one **earned** beat.
- **Anti-bathos:** after humor, **one line** that restores stakes (time, danger, cost) so the room doesn’t feel silly.

## Optimism playbook

- Show **what someone does** despite fear (preparation, kindness, stubbornness).
- **Connection:** one true line of dialogue, touch, or shared glance.
- **Small wins** that don’t solve everything but prove the world isn’t only loss.
- **Texture of hope:** specific sensory or procedural detail (food, repair, plan, ritual)—not abstract reassurance.

## Prose craft (anti-monotone)

- **Sentence variety:** mix short punches with longer rhythmic lines; avoid same-length blocks.
- **Lexical warmth:** choose words a friend would use at the table—precise, not stiff.
- **Emotional micro-beats:** a beat of body, breath, or silence between information dumps.
- **Drift detector:** watch for **passive stacks** and **nominalization** (“implementation of mitigation” → who does what, now?).
- **Register exception:** bureaucratic or corporate diction is fine **in dialogue or in-world documents**, not as default narrator voice.

## Scene recipe (quick)

1. Anchor in **concrete image or action**.
2. State **stakes** in human terms.
3. Allow **cost** to land.
4. Offer **orientation**—what’s possible, who cares, what’s funny-tragic in the gap.
5. End beat on **motion** (choice, change, hook)—not a filing cabinet.

## Micro-examples (voice direction, not canonical text)

*Report-speak (avoid):* “The subject experienced a negative outcome following the encounter.”

*Warmer, same fact:* “It went wrong. You know it did—the silence after says it.”

*Grim without hope (avoid):* endless suffering with no one trying anything.

*Dark + optimistic stance:* “The odds are obscene. She smiles anyway—small, useless, *hers*—and starts counting supplies.”

## Failure modes to catch in edit

- **Report-speak:** neutral voice, passive constructions, jargon; nothing lands when read aloud.
- **Grimdark without hope:** suffering stacks with no tether (relationship, value, goal) or variation—numbness, not dread.
- **Humor undercuts stakes:** jokes right after a death blow with no beat, or winking that the story doesn’t care.
- **Toxic positivity:** bright language on horror reads dismissive.
- **Inconsistent narrator:** asides vanish then return as random snark—two writers in one passage.

## Revision checklist

- Would a **friend-DM** say it this way out loud, or only a memo?
- Is there **one** moment of **connection, agency, or dry wit** where humans are present?
- After a dark beat, is there **contrast** (breath, humanity, humor, or hope-texture)?
- Does humor **punch up or sideways**, not undercut the cost we just established?
- Any **bathos**? If a joke lands wrong, restore **stakes** then retry subtler.
- Scan for **passive voice / nominalization** and replace with actors and verbs.

## Glossary (short)

- **Gallows grace:** humor or warmth that admits the worst without pretending it isn’t there.
- **Pressure-release humor:** tension down a notch; not a genre shift to comedy.
- **Agency return:** after pain, the prose points to what *can still be chosen*.
- **Table-safe:** dark content framed so the reader can engage without numbness.

## Optional reflection prompts (for authors)

Use in critique or solo pass—not required in shipped game text.

- After this beat, does the reader know **what matters** and **what they might do**?
- If I removed the joke, would the scene still **earn** a breath another way?
- What **true detail** shows hope without lying about the world?
- After your darkest scene in a stretch, what is the **smallest** true detail that signals “we’re still in a world where X matters” without resolving the horror—and would one line of dry wit sit better **before** or **after** that beat, and why?
- If you read three consecutive passages aloud, do they sound like the **same trusted guide**—or like alternating novelist, policy memo, and stand-up? What **one rule** would keep those voices from fighting?
