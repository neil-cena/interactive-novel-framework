# PART I — THE PROTAGONIST

---

## Chapter 1: The Protagonist

### 1.1 Identity

- Member of the **Measured Scholars** faction
- Junior field evaluator
- Trained to analyze systems and recommend "optimal exchanges"
- Specialization: **Structural / Systems Alchemy**
- **Name:** Vael (used in narrative text; the player inhabits this role in second person)

### 1.2 Job Description

Sent into the field to:

- Assess incidents
- Determine the cause of harm
- Recommend solutions

The protagonist is NOT sent to save people. They are sent to determine **the most efficient outcome given constraints.** Their job is analysis, not heroism.

### 1.3 Starting Beliefs

- "If all variables are accounted for, the correct decision becomes obvious."
- Emotion introduces error
- Sacrifice is acceptable if the calculations justify it
- Systems matter more than individuals
- Outcomes define correctness

**The protagonist is NOT evil.** They are incomplete. They genuinely believe in optimization and think they are helping people by finding the best possible outcome. They just don't understand what "best" really means yet.

### 1.4 Blind Spots

1. **Underestimates human cost** — Sees numbers and outcomes, not lived experience. A death is a variable, not a tragedy.
2. **Overestimates control** — Believes systems behave predictably. They don't. People are not variables.
3. **Believes in "clean decisions"** — Thinks optimal solutions exist. The world has none.

### 1.5 Character Arc (Five Stages)

1. **Stage 1 — Confident Analyst:** Trusts calculations. Believes in objectivity. Everything is a system to optimize.
2. **Stage 2 — Discomfort:** Sees outcomes that feel wrong but cannot explain the feeling. The calculations were "correct" but the result is terrible.
3. **Stage 3 — Fracture:** Makes a "correct" decision that has terrible consequences. The math said it was right. People suffered anyway.
4. **Stage 4 — Re-evaluation:** Begins factoring in people, trust, uncertainty, human factors. Struggles with the tension between optimization and compassion.
5. **Stage 5 — Integration:** Learns that optimization is not enough. Cost must be understood, not just measured. Becomes someone who uses analysis AS A TOOL rather than AS A WORLDVIEW.

### 1.6 Core Conflict

**Optimization vs. humanity.** "I know what the numbers say is right — but I don't have the power to enforce it, and maybe the numbers aren't enough."

### 1.7 Faction Relationships

- **Scholars (home faction):** Expect precision. Reward efficiency. Discourage emotional deviation. The protagonist fits here — at first.
- **Workers:** Distrust them. See them as detached. "Another calculator who doesn't understand real work."
- **State/CEA:** Values their recommendations. May try to weaponize their analysis for political purposes.
- **Radicals:** See them as weak or naive. "You calculate while the world burns."

### 1.8 Loadout

**Field Scholar archetype:**

- Pre-inscribed plates (multiple functions, fast deployment)
- Measurement tools (rulers, calipers — for analysis)
- Limited fuel supply
- Strengths: adaptable, analytical, can handle varied situations
- Weaknesses: requires setup time, limited endurance, dependent on supplies

# PART II — STORY

## Chapter 2: Equivalent Ashes — Story Summary

### 2.1 Setting

**City:** Orvish — the republic's largest port city, in the Harbor Cities region of Caldris.

**District:** Karet Bay — the lower docks, a district with a documented history of being on the wrong end of institutional decisions. Three generations ago, a trade consortium's alchemical water-flow redirection destroyed its freshwater supply and fisheries; the resulting legal principle classifying environmental harm as harm to people is now cited in every environmental assessment. The district remembers.

### 2.2 Inciting Situation

A harbor crane in Karet Bay's loading district is operating at 23% above its rated structural load capacity. Maintenance logs show no authorized reinforcement work. Fuel records show normal consumption. Nothing explains the numbers.

Vael is assigned as a junior field evaluator to assess the crane, determine the cause of the anomaly, and file a report. They are explicitly told: observe and calculate. That's all.

### 2.3 What Vael Discovers

The crane anomaly is not an accident. It is the visible symptom of a systemic material redirection: the CEA has been quietly rerouting high-quality maintenance materials away from Karet Bay's infrastructure — with correct paperwork, official justification, and the full support of institutional process — to fund a transit rail project elsewhere. The district's buildings, seawall, and equipment have been running on degraded materials for seven months.

The people doing the unofficial reinforcement on the crane (using cheap, dangerous materials and improvised circles) are dock workers who noticed the infrastructure failing and tried to fix it themselves. They are not reckless. They are the only people who have been doing maintenance at all.

The deeper threat: the district's seawall is also compromised. If it fails, the lower docks flood. Six thousand people are at risk.

### 2.4 The Central Conflict

Vael has everything they need to file a correct, accurate, well-documented report. They can identify the cause of the anomaly, name the responsible parties, and recommend remediation through proper channels.

The problem: the proper channels take time, and the seawall may not.

The central choice the story forces: **do the right work (accurate report, proper process), or do the right thing (act now, pay the cost yourself)?** Vael's training says these are the same. They are not.

### 2.5 Key Characters

- **Senior Scholar Mireth** — Vael's supervisor. Has watched this pattern three times in nineteen years. Sends Vael with a warning she can't quite say out loud: "Good work isn't the same as right work. Those are different things."
- **Kaelen** — Lead dock worker, informal crew leader. Practical, suspicious of the Scholar's presence, but not unreasonable. Has been managing the infrastructure failure on the crew's own resources.
- **Elara** — Local practical alchemist, not Scholars-trained. Works directly with materials and people rather than through formal assessment processes. Shows Vael what alchemy looks like when it isn't filtered through institutional distance.
- **Dima** — A kid on Kaelen's crew who is paying close attention to everything. Writes things down.
- **Overseer Toris** (CEA) — The administrator behind the material redirection. Not a monster. Has a justification for every decision that sounds reasonable and produces outcomes like this.

### 2.6 Endings

The story has multiple endings depending on Vael's choices. The core tension resolves differently based on whether Vael:

- Files a correct report and trusts the process
- Acts outside their authority to protect the district directly (at personal cost — distortion to their hands)
- Attempts to work with the dock crew rather than around them

All endings are valid. The story does not punish the player for choosing institutional correctness — it shows what that choice costs. It does not reward personal sacrifice uncritically — it shows what that costs too.

The "Costly Good Deed" ending (Vael uses themselves as a conduit to hold the seawall through a chemical surge, resulting in permanent crystalline distortion to their hands) is the thematic centerpiece: *"The cost was real. The cost was yours. In a world where someone always pays — choosing to pay yourself is not the only form of courage. But it is the most honest one."*

### 2.7 Themes Being Tested

- **Equivalent exchange in practice:** The same law that justifies institutional decisions also justifies personal sacrifice. The difference is in who chooses the distribution.
- **Correct process vs. correct outcome:** Vael's training is genuinely useful and genuinely insufficient.
- **The limits of analysis:** You can calculate correctly and still cause harm. You can act on incomplete information and still do the right thing.
- **Institutional complicity:** Nobody in this story is a villain. Everyone is operating within sanctioned systems. The harm is systemic, not personal.

# PART III — NARRATIVE ARCHITECTURE

This story is built according to the **four-layer narrative architecture** specified in [`NARRATIVE_ARCHITECTURE.md`](NARRATIVE_ARCHITECTURE.md). That document is the canonical ruleset. What follows are the story-specific applications of each layer to Equivalent Ashes.

---

## Chapter 3: Equivalent Ashes — Architecture Notes

### 3.1 Layer 1 — The Main String (Mandatory Pearls)

The following beats are pearls. Every player passes through all of them:

1. **The Assignment** — Vael receives the crane assessment from Mireth.
2. **The Crane Anomaly** — Vael observes the crane operating above rated capacity.
3. **The Discovery** — Vael uncovers the systemic material redirection behind the anomaly.
4. **The Seawall Crisis** — The compromised seawall becomes an immediate threat.
5. **The Final Choice** — Vael decides how to respond to the crisis.

Everything between these pearls is hub-and-spoke exploration space. The argument of the story — that correct process and correct outcome are not the same thing — requires all five pearls to be witnessed.

### 3.2 Layer 2 — Hub and Spoke in Karet Bay

**Hubs:** The main hubs in this story are `post_crane_pattern` (the open investigation phase), `track_disturbance` (the purifier district exploration), and `race_to_seawall` (the final approach). These are the nodes from which multiple directions extend.

**Short spokes:** The market terrace, the loadout review, looking out the window at the city. Texture and resource variation only.

**Long spokes (current):**
- *Dima's notebook → theater route* — Leads into Petra's underground theater and the district's cultural memory. Returns Vael with the `seawall_analysis` item and a different emotional frame for the crisis.
- *Cat colony route* — Reached only through a specific flag + item + flag combination. A long spoke that explores the district's stranger unofficial governance. Returns via the purifier.
- *Authority overplay route* — A loss spoke. Vael escalates formally without sufficient standing. Has two exit opportunities before `death_authorization_revoked`.

**Alternative string anchors (characters to watch):**
- **Dima** — Already seeded at `dima_encounter`. His notebook route and theater involvement make him the most developed alternative string anchor in the current build.
- **Elara** — Seeded at `meet_elara`. A practical alchemist outside institutional structures. Sufficient content exists for an alternative string grounded in craft rather than investigation.
- **Kaelen** — Seeded at `meet_kaelen`. An alternative string grounded in the dock workers' world is possible once sufficient engagement flags are set.

### 3.3 Layer 3 — State Variables and Their Meanings

The four world stats in this story have specific narrative meanings. All state changes must be justifiable against these meanings:

| Stat | Narrative Meaning | High Value | Low Value |
|------|-------------------|------------|-----------|
| `vaelEnergy` | Vael's physical and alchemical reserves | Costly options are available | Sacrifice ending's cost is higher; some active choices close |
| `districtStability` | How structurally and socially intact Karet Bay is | CEA resolution is politically viable | Crisis is acute; institutional process has run out of time |
| `stateChaos` | How destabilized the CEA's institutional grip has become | Radical options become visible | Only official channels remain viable |
| `communityCost` | How much the district has been made to absorb | Kaelen's crew is mobilized; community help is available | The district cannot organize in time |

**State gate audit (current story):** At `final_assessment`, the CEA-style controlled-collapse options split on `districtStability` (≤55 vs >55 so only one of the two labels shows at a time; both route to the same epilogue chain). The radical overload option uses `stateChaos >= 20` (starting value `10`, so the player must raise chaos through play—e.g. confrontation beats, Red Balance contact, or related choices). A separate radical branch requires `transit_anomaly`. Sacrifice and collective routes use always-on or item/reputation gates. Stats still need periodic review as new nodes are added so no ending becomes trivially guaranteed without fiction support.

**Items that must gate content (current build):**
- `alchemical_chalk` — already checked at `crane_collapse`
- `district_map` — already checked at `track_disturbance`
- `seawall_analysis` — already checked at `toris_reveal`
- `energy_tonic`, `cea_authorization`, `energy_readings`, `worker_testimony`, `crane_report`, `transit_anomaly`, `toris_orders` — currently given but not checked. These must gate content in a future pass or be removed.

### 3.4 Layer 4 — Alternative Strings in This Story

This story is a short introduction. Alternative strings are seeded but not yet fully implemented. The following are the intended alternative strings:

**Alternative String A — "The Chronicler" (anchored by Dima)**

*Unlock threshold:* `saw_the_city` flag set + `dima_notebook` visited + `theater_revelation` completed + `communityCost >= 35`

*Premise:* Vael becomes absorbed in Dima's project of documenting what the CEA has done to the district — not through official reports but through testimony, physical evidence, and the kind of record-keeping that outlasts institutions. The crane assessment becomes secondary.

*Commitment point:* After `theater_revelation`, if Vael chooses to stay with Petra's troupe rather than racing to the seawall. The seawall crisis resolves without Vael's intervention (the world stat shift reflects this). Vael's main string window closes.

*Soft closure:* Vael helps Dima complete and distribute the district chronicle. The crisis resolved badly without them. But the record exists. Someone will read it. The cost was real; the choice was theirs.

**Alternative String B — "The Craft" (anchored by Elara)**

*Unlock threshold:* `elara_met` flag + having visited `purifier_choice_modify` or `purifier_choice_destroy` + `vaelEnergy <= 60` at `seek_answers`

*Premise:* Vael, depleted and increasingly disillusioned with institutional analysis, is drawn into Elara's way of working — direct, material, relationship-based. The alternative string explores what alchemy looks like when it isn't filtered through credentials and reports.

*Commitment point:* Vael accepts Elara's offer to work on the district's infrastructure directly, without CEA authorization. The formal Scholar assignment is abandoned. The commitment point is the moment Vael begins unauthorized alchemical work on a structure that isn't the crane.

*Hard or soft closure:* Depends on choices made within the string. If Vael burns their remaining energy on unauthorized work, hard closure (distortion). If Vael is careful and strategic, soft closure: Vael remains in the district, Scholars-credential revoked, doing real work.

*Note: The Chronicler thread (`theater_revelation` → `chronicler_commitment` → `ending_chronicler_v2`) is implemented in `nodes.csv`; the full arc described above (including every threshold in the unlock list) may still be tuned. "The Craft" remains a design target rather than a complete playable string.*
